Keep the Edge. Respect the Craft.

A premium kitchen blade is only as good as its apex. We provide precision water-stone sharpening and restoration without the destructive friction heat of motorized grinders.

Workshop Discipline

Our 4-Stage Cold-Honing Protocol

Why culinary professionals entrust their Japanese and European cutlery to our studio.

01

Intake & Inspection

We inspect the edge under optical magnification to detect micro-chipping, apex rolling, blade dishing (loss of flat profile), and tip fractures. We document existing bevel angles before beginning work.

02

Primary Sharpening

Blades are ground on low-RPM, water-cooled ceramic wheels and coarse #400/#1,000 synthetic whetstones. The continuous water bath absorbs all thermal energy, preserving the blade's molecular temper.

03

Progressive Honing

The edge progresses through fine Japanese water stones (#3,000 to #6,000 grit) to align the scratch pattern, followed by double horsehide stropping charged with sub-micron diamond emulsion.

04

Test & Preservation

Every knife must pass cleanly through free-hanging culinary tissue and shaved paper with zero snagging. The blade is thoroughly cleaned, coated in Japanese camellia oil, and sleeved in a protective card saya.

Intake Scope

Accepted Culinary Cutlery

Our workshop specializes strictly in kitchen and culinary cutlery. We accept:

  • Western Chef Knives & French Profiles
  • Japanese Gyuto, Santoku, Nakiri, and Bunka
  • Paring, Petty, and Utility Knives
  • Serrated Bread Knives (individually honed)
  • Heavy Kitchen Cleavers & Chuka Bocho
  • Single-Bevel Deba and Yanagiba
We do not service non-culinary tools, folding pocket blades, or outdoor knives.
